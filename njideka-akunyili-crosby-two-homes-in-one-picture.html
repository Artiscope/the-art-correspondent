Njideka Akunyili Crosby: Two Homes in One Picture

By The Art Correspondent. Published 19 November 2026.

From across the room it is a quiet domestic scene. Up close, the walls and floors turn out to be made of hundreds of photographs.

A couple at a table. A woman on a sofa. A room with a patterned floor. Njideka Akunyili Crosby paints ordinary moments at home. Then you step closer and the surfaces dissolve into images: faces, magazine pages, family snapshots, printed into the picture itself.

Enugu to Los Angeles

She was born in Enugu, Nigeria, in 1983, and moved to the United States at sixteen to continue her education. She studied at the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts and at Yale, and now lives and works in Los Angeles.

How the pictures are built

Her works combine acrylic paint, charcoal, coloured pencil, fabric, collage and photo transfer. The transfers are made with acetone, which lifts a printed image and sets it down on the paper, slightly softened.

The source material is personal. She uses photographs she has taken in Nigeria, pictures from family albums and pages from popular Nigerian magazines. Laid side by side, they become wallpaper, clothing and floors inside the painted room.

Two worlds, one room

The Carnegie Corporation, which named her among its Great Immigrants in 2020, describes work that ranges from the legacy of colonialism in Africa to the experience of being an immigrant in America. What is striking is how gently it is done. Nothing is announced. A Nigerian pop star sits in the pattern of a dress. A family portrait is half hidden in the floor.

The effect is close to how memory works. The present scene is in front of you, and the past is in everything around it.

Recognition

In 2017 she received a MacArthur Fellowship, often called the genius grant. In 2018 the National Portrait Gallery in London showed her series The Beautyful Ones. She is represented by Victoria Miro and David Zwirner.

Why it matters

Many artists have pictured life between two countries as a kind of split. Akunyili Crosby pictures it as a layering. Both homes are present at once, in the same room, on the same sheet of paper. That is a more accurate account of how a great many people now live.
