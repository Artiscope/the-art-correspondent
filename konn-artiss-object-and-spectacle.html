Konn Artiss and the Relationship Between Object and Spectacle

By The Art Correspondent. Published 19 November 2026.

A block of ice, a parody newspaper, a mannequin on a canal. Konn Artiss makes objects that are built to disappear, and events that are built to stay.

On the morning visitors arrived at Frieze London in October 2025, five large blocks of ice were standing outside the main entrance. Art Plugged reported that each weighed 40 stone and each held a miniature effigy of a famous name: Damien Hirst, Andy Warhol, Tracey Emin, Jeff Koons and KAWS.

The work was called ANTI-FRIEZE. Then it melted. By the account in Art Plugged, what remained was puddles, bananas and a crowd caught between curiosity and discomfort.

An artist nobody has met

Konn Artiss is anonymous. The name first drew wide attention in June 2025. FAD Magazine reported a stunt in Washington DC built around The Washington Ghost, a parody newspaper aimed at Jeff Bezos shortly before his wedding in Venice. FAD noted that the name Konn Artiss had surfaced on social media with the sign-off "Trust Me".

The action then moved to Venice itself. GB News reported mannequins of Bezos and his fiancée floating along the Grand Canal in a gondola, and said more than 10,000 copies of the newspaper had been handed out.

The object

Look at what these works are made of. Frozen water. Newsprint. Shop mannequins. Fruit. None of it is meant to last, and none of it can be hung on a wall and sold in the usual way. The ice is the clearest case. It is a sculpture with a clock inside it.

The bananas are harder to read. It is difficult not to think of Maurizio Cattelan, who taped one to a wall at an art fair in 2019 and watched it become one of the most discussed artworks of the decade. That reading is ours, not the artist's.

The spectacle

If the object vanishes, where is the artwork? Mostly in what happens around it: the people who stop, the photographs they take, the reports that follow. Art Plugged read ANTI-FRIEZE as a comment on value, hype and decay, and on an art market that prizes permanence. The melting made that argument without a word of wall text.

This is the tension the work lives on. It mocks spectacle and depends on it completely. An intervention that nobody photographs has, in practical terms, not happened.

What is left

What stays behind is the record and the question. With an anonymous artist there is no biography to lean on, and with a melted sculpture there is no object to revisit. We are left to decide whether the event said something true. In October 2025, outside the tent where art is bought and sold, a lot of people stopped to watch famous names drip away. That is worth reporting.
