From Gallery Wall to Hotel Wall: The Gallery in Residence

By The Art Correspondent. Published 19 November 2026.

A hotel used to buy its art once and hang it for twenty years. Now some are borrowing a gallery instead.

Walk into most hotels and the art is part of the fit-out, chosen with the carpet and expected to last as long. A different arrangement has been spreading. A gallery takes over the walls, hangs a show, and a few months later hangs another.

How it works

The idea is borrowed from the artist residency, where a maker is given a place to work for a set time. Here it is the gallery, or one of its artists, that takes up residence. The hotel gets a changing display it did not have to buy. The gallery gets a second set of walls and a new audience walking past them.

A west London example

In March 2025 the Dorsett hotel in Shepherds Bush announced a partnership with Grove Gallery and named its first artist in residence: Silent Mike.

Art Plugged reported that the show, Chromatic Shift, placed fifteen works through the hotel's main bar and restaurant and opened to the public on 28 March. It was a change of direction for the artist. His 2023 debut at Quantus Gallery in London, Introspective, had been entirely in black and white. The new pieces were in full colour.

The method is worth describing. According to the same report, each piece is built from machine-cut wooden circles with hand-sanded surfaces, finished in layers of paint, primer and a glossy paste. The hotel described intricate geometric patterns combined with elements of classical sculpture, and Art Plugged named Michelangelo and Bernini as reference points.

The hotel's announcement made two promises that matter. The artist would be present to talk about his process, and more artists would follow.

Not a one-off

The same gallery lists a rolling residency at South Place Hotel in east London, with work by Zen Xander, Frank Canvas and Robi Walters. It records a three-month Zara Muse exhibition at a venue called Shanghai Noir in 2024. FAD Magazine reported a year-long display of her portraits at Fairmont Windsor Park.

Residency or decoration

What separates a residency from simply renting out pictures? Three things, we would suggest.

The first is change. If the work never rotates, it is décor. The second is the artist. A residency should mean the maker is named, credited and now and then in the room. The third is information. A guest should be able to find out what they are looking at without asking at reception.

What it means for artists

For an artist early in a career, a hotel offers something a small gallery cannot: hundreds of people a day with time on their hands. For the visitor it removes the awkwardness some feel about walking into a gallery at all.

The cost is control. A bar is noisy, the lighting is for atmosphere and nobody came for the art. Work shown there has to hold its own without help.

That is not a bad test. A picture that can stop someone on the way to breakfast is doing something right.
