Zen Xander: One Name, Many Hands

By The Art Correspondent. Published 19 November 2026.

A lion in a virtual reality headset eating salad. The joke lands at once. The harder question is who made it.

Most artist profiles begin with a person. This one cannot, because the accounts of who Zen Xander is do not agree.

Two versions

When Grove Gallery in Fitzrovia opened Society Falling in June 2024, Art Plugged introduced Zen Xander as an anonymous street artist known for a satirical approach to fine art, moving from the street into the gallery. Artsy lists the show as a first solo exhibition, running from 26 June to 31 July 2024.

The artist's own website tells it differently. It says Zen Xander is not a single artist but a small group of painters, illustrators, designers and writers who share one aim: to make art that is warm, funny and easy to recognise yourself in.

We cannot settle which account is the fuller one, and we will not guess. It is worth noticing that both can be true of a name. A signature can belong to a studio as easily as to a hand.

The work

What is not in doubt is what the pictures look like. Animals are placed in human situations and left to carry the argument. Art Plugged described VR Vegan, in which a lion wears a virtual reality headset while eating a bowl of salad, as a jab at the gap between real and virtual life and at the contradictions of modern diets.

The method comes from the street. Artsy records the works in Society Falling, among them Nut Hacker, Drone Cat and Always a Cheetah, as hand-cut stencil and spray paint on plywood, each roughly a metre across.

Why animals

Satire needs a little distance. Put a person in the picture and the viewer starts asking who it is meant to be. Put a cheetah or a cat there and everyone is free to see themselves. It is an old trick, as old as fables, and it suits an artist, or a group of artists, who would rather not be the story.

Out of the gallery

The work has not stayed in Fitzrovia. Grove Gallery's own record of its exhibitions lists Zen Xander pieces at South Place Hotel in east London, alongside Frank Canvas and Robi Walters, and a continuation of Society Falling at a Dorsett hotel in London.

An anonymous name, a recognisable style and a joke that reads in two seconds. Whoever holds the spray can, the formula is clear.
