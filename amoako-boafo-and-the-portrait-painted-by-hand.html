Amoako Boafo and the Portrait Painted by Hand

By The Art Correspondent. Published 19 November 2026.

He paints faces and hands with his own fingers. You can see the touch in every one of them.

Look closely at the skin in an Amoako Boafo portrait. It is made of swirls and ridges of oil paint, blues and browns pushed around by a fingertip. The clothes and the background are flat and calm. All the movement is in the body.

Accra to Vienna

Boafo was born in Accra, Ghana, on 10 May 1984. He studied at the Ghanatta College of Art and Design there, and later at the Academy of Fine Arts in Vienna. Artnet records that he moved to Vienna in 2014 and began a series of portraits of Black people he met in the city.

Vienna gave him two things. One was the example of Egon Schiele and the painters of the Vienna Secession, with their twisting, expressive figures. The other, by several accounts, was the experience of seeing how rarely Black subjects appeared on the walls around him.

The fingers

According to Colección SOLO, it was in 2017 that he decided to start painting with his fingers. The technique is selective. One detailed account notes that he finger-paints exposed skin in oil and uses brushes and paper transfer for much of the clothing, objects and backgrounds.

The effect is that touch itself becomes part of the meaning. These are portraits of friends, family and people he admires, and the paint on their faces has been laid there by hand.

A fast rise

The art market noticed quickly. Ocula reports that in 2020 his painting The Lemon Bathing Suit, made the year before, sold at auction for £675,000. The same year he worked with Dior on a fashion collection.

Museums followed. Soul of Black Folks opened at the Museum of the African Diaspora in San Francisco in 2021 and travelled to the Denver Art Museum in 2023. In 2024 the Belvedere in Vienna showed more than fifty works under the title Proper Love, hanging some beside Schiele and Klimt.

Home again

It would be easy to tell this as a story about prices. A better one is about place. Recent profiles give Accra as the centre of his studio and of the cultural projects he supports, with Vienna still part of the picture. The painter who left to study has gone back to build something.
