Artist Stories

The lives and working practices of the artists we follow.

  • Njideka Akunyili Crosby: Two Homes in One Picture

    From across the room it is a quiet domestic scene. Up close, the walls and floors turn out to be made of hundreds of photographs.

  • Amoako Boafo and the Portrait Painted by Hand

    He paints faces and hands with his own fingers. You can see the touch in every one of them.

  • Amanza Smith Starts Again on Canvas

    Television audiences know the name already. The paintings ask to be looked at without it.

  • Robi Walters: Petals Cut from Broken Records

    He cuts petals from broken vinyl and old packaging, and arranges them into flowers that seem to burst off the wall.

  • Frank Canvas and the One-Line Joke

    Some pictures need a wall text. A Frank Canvas needs a title of three words or fewer.

  • Zen Xander: One Name, Many Hands

    A lion in a virtual reality headset eating salad. The joke lands at once. The harder question is who made it.

  • Konn Artiss and the Relationship Between Object and Spectacle

    A block of ice, a parody newspaper, a mannequin on a canal. Konn Artiss makes objects that are built to disappear, and events that are built to stay.

  • Zara Muse and the Physical Language of Portraiture

    She paints with a palette knife and a spatula, and nothing else. The result is portraiture you read almost as much by its surface as by its likeness.
