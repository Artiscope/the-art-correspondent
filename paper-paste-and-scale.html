Paper, Paste and Scale: The Craft Behind JR's Street Portraits

By The Art Correspondent. Published 19 November 2026.

A camera, a big printer, a bucket of paste and a wall. JR's method is simple enough that he has given it away.

The French artist JR, born in Paris in 1983, describes himself with a word of his own: photograffeur, part photographer and part graffiti writer. He takes portraits, prints them very large in black and white and pastes them onto buildings.

Three ingredients

The method has barely changed. First a photograph, usually a face, shot close. Then the print, on plain paper in strips large enough to cover a wall. Then the paste, brushed on by hand, often by teams of volunteers.

Each part is cheap. None of it is permanent. Rain and sun take the paper off within months. That is not a flaw in the work. A pasted portrait is closer to an event than to a monument.

Why so big

Scale does the arguing. A passport-sized face asks nothing of you. The same face six storeys high is hard to pass without wondering who it is.

His early series Portraits of a Generation, made between 2004 and 2006, showed young people from the housing estates around Paris in huge format. For Face 2 Face, the Brooklyn Museum records, he and his collaborators pasted large portraits side by side on both the Israeli and Palestinian sides of the separation wall.

Giving the method away

In 2011 JR won the TED Prize, worth 100,000 dollars, and used it to start the Inside Out Project. The idea was to hand his process to anyone who wanted it.

It works like this. A group takes its own portraits and sends them in. JR's studio prints them as posters and ships them back. The group pastes them up wherever it chooses. Phaidon's account adds that the team later built photobooth trucks that take a picture and print a large poster on the spot.

The numbers are large. By August 2022 there had been more than 2,300 group actions, with nearly half a million posters sent to 148 countries.

What the craft teaches

There is a lesson here for anyone who thinks ambitious art needs expensive materials. JR's kit could be bought on any high street. What makes the work is the decision about whose face goes where, and the labour of many people with brushes and ladders.

Paper, paste and scale. The rest is permission, or the lack of it.
