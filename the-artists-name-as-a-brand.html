The Artist's Name as a Brand

By The Art Correspondent. Published 19 November 2026.

Zara Muse, Zen Xander, Konn Artiss, Vhils, JR, Mr Dot. Count how many of the artists in these pages work under a name they chose.

Artists have always signed their work. What is newer is how many now sign it with a name that is not on their passport.

A name as a shield

Some choose a working name for privacy. Zara Muse is the professional name of Alexandra Johnson, who has said it gives her a degree of separation between her life and her work. The paintings can travel to hotels, auctions and other countries. The person can stay at home.

Others go further and withhold the person altogether. Konn Artiss is anonymous. JR is a pseudonym, and his full identity has never been officially confirmed. In both cases the secrecy has a practical side, since much of the work is done in public without asking.

A name as a tag

For artists who began in graffiti, the chosen name comes first. Vhils is the tag Alexandre Farto used as a teenage graffiti writer around Lisbon. A tag is a signature designed to be written fast and recognised at a distance. That is also a fair description of a logo.

A name as a group

A name does not have to belong to one person. Zen Xander has been described in the press as an anonymous street artist. The artist's own website says it is a small collective of painters, illustrators, designers and writers working toward a shared style.

That is less strange than it sounds. The workshops of the old masters turned out paintings under one name with many hands involved. El Anatsui's vast hangings are assembled by teams. The single signature has often stood for a studio.

A name as a character

Then there are names that work as characters. Clarke Reynolds, who makes art from braille, also appears as Mr Dot, a figure he uses to reach children. The name does a job the artist's own could not.

When the name becomes the product

Once a name is recognised, it can be attached to things. Limited edition prints, signed and numbered, are the simplest case. Hotel residencies are another. Fashion is a third: in 2020 Amoako Boafo worked with Dior on a menswear collection that carried the textures of his portraits onto clothes.

None of this is new. Andy Warhol understood sixty years ago that an artist's name could work like a company's. What has changed is the speed. A name can now go from a wall to a product in a couple of years.

What to make of it

It is tempting to be cynical. A chosen name can look like marketing, and sometimes it is.

But there is another way to see it. A working name lets the art be judged a little more on its own. It protects people who would not otherwise show their work. It lets several people speak with one voice. The name on the label is a decision, like every other decision in the picture, and it is worth asking what it is doing there.
